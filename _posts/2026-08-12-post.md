  ## Engineered receptors for soluble cellular communication and disease sensing

Piraner DI and Abedi MH et al. modified the SNIPRs to detect soluble ligands like TGF-β and VEGF to activate CAR-T cells in the tumor microenvironment. Such an approach enhances the safety of CAR-T cell therapeutics. The SNIPRs lack an LNR domain, which is critical to the activation of the notch receptor via a force-induced mechanism, indicating they adapt an alternative pathway for receptor activation.

Unlike synNotch receptors, which require membrane-tethered ligands, soluble SNIPRs are activated by ligand-induced dimerisation, followed by endocytosis and pH-dependent intramembrane cleavage in the endosome that releases a transcription factor. The authors installed scFvs against TGF-β and VEGF, as well as FGF2 and IFN-γ, and showed that the TGF-β SNIPR is specific to the active form of the cytokine, which is enriched in the tumour microenvironment. When used to drive CAR expression, the SNIPR–CAR circuits localise T cell activity to tumours that secrete these factors. In xenograft models of lung adenocarcinoma and melanoma, SNIPR-gated HER2 CAR-T cells controlled tumour growth while avoiding the on-target, off-tumour toxicity seen with constitutively expressed CARs. They further engineered bio-orthogonal SNIPRs with computationally designed heterodimers, creating private communication channels between engineered cells that are independent of natural signalling pathways.

<h3>Questions to be addressed</h3>
1. How robust is soluble SNIPR activation in tumours that secrete only low or heterogeneous levels of TGF-β or VEGF?
2. What is the long-term safety profile of SNIPR-gated CAR-T cells in immunocompetent models, given that TGF-β and VEGF are also present in healthy tissues?
3. Can multi-SNIPR circuits that integrate several soluble or surface inputs further restrict CAR activity and reduce residual off-tumour toxicity?
